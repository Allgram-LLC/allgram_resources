# Privacy's a birthright, not a perk

Have you ever seen an online ad for something you were just talking
about?

Those digital déjà-vus are kind of scary, right? The thing is, they're a
feature of social media, not a bug.

So, are they listening to us?

In 2016, Facebook published a blog post stating, "Facebook does not use
your phone's microphone to inform ads or to change what you see in News
Feed."

In 2018, Mark Zuckerberg testified before the Senate that our mobiles
aren't listening.

In 2025, Adam Mosseri (the Instagram CEO) denied Instagram actively
listens to our conversations, with Facebook's terms and conditions
saying much the same.

Mosseri's statement, however, came just as Meta announced that --- for
Facebook, Instagram and Threads users --- data from your interactions
with their AI will help push more ads your way.

Lucky you.

Thanks to those algos, they don't even need to listen to our
conversations.

## Social media is social surveillance

With centralized social media, your data isn't yours --- it's theirs ---
hoarded on corporate servers, sold to advertisers and stolen by hackers.

Centralized social media invites disaster, always one exploit away from
your privacy being stolen. Throw governments and algos into the mix and,
before you know it, everything's compromised.

Don't forget the Instagram hack in 2025, Twitter 2022, LinkedIn and
Facebook in 2021, Sina Weibo 2020, Yahoo 2013 and 2014, MySpace, Tumblr,
VK --- billions compromised by centralized data vaults that put us all
at risk.

In a world of infinite scrolls, those ads echoing your private chats
aren't coincidence, they're surveillance --- and you're the product,
you're the merch.

Creepy? Sure.

Inevitable? Not anymore.
