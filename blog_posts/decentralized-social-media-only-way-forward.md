Your data isn’t safe.

We all know it, so why accept it?

Take the latest Instagram [hack](https://cybersecuritynews.com/instagram-data-leak-exposes-sensitive-info-of-17-5m-accounts/). In late 2025, hackers scraped sensitive data from over 17 million accounts, including full names, usernames, emails, phone numbers and (partial) locations. They did it by exploiting public APIs and a flawed password reset system.

Instagram might [downplay](https://x.com/instagram/status/2010202301886238822) it, but the data’s already out there, ready for phishing, ID theft and account takeovers. We’re sitting ducks because one centralized giant couldn't secure the data.

The dirty secret? This isn’t a bug, it’s a feature.

Flashback to 2018, Facebook and Cambridge Analytica, the data of 87 million users [siphoned](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/article/cambridge-analytica-controversy/) without consent for political games.  
In 2020, Sina Weibo [lost](https://wolfesystems.com.au/the-digital-frontier-unravelling-the-2020-sina-weibo-breach/) 538 million names, phones and locations.  
Then there’s the 2021 Facebook [scrape](https://www.wired.com/story/facebook-data-leak-500-million-users-phone-numbers/) exposing 533 million profiles, names, numbers and emails.

2022 was Twitter’s turn — 5.4 million accounts [leaked](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2022/08/twitter-confirmed-july-2022-data-breach-affecting-5-4m-users) through zero-day API abuse, then another 200 million [more](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-64153381) for good measure.

LinkedIn? A 2021 [breach](https://www.forbes.com/sites/leemathews/2021/06/29/details-on-700-million-linkedin-users-for-sale-on-notorious-hacking-forum/) compromised 700 million records, emails and locations.

Yahoo [2013](https://www.huntress.com/threat-library/data-breach/yahoo-data-breach) and [2014](https://digitalcommons.wcl.american.edu/aulr/vol66/iss5/3/),  
[MySpace](https://www.huntress.com/threat-library/data-breach/myspace-data-breach),  
[Tumblr](https://www.sgllp.co.uk/insurance-solution/three-years-after-a-major-security-breach-at-tumblr-65m-passwords-and-emails-for-sale/),  
[VK](https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/11065-390-million-users-impacted-by-alleged-russian-messaging-app-data-breach-as-vk-denies-any-hack) —  
the roster stretches on like the [Star Wars scrawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_opening_crawl), billions compromised by centralized data vaults that put us all at risk.
